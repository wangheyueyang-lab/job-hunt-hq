# English writing guide

## Short intro paragraph (motivation boxes, summaries, email openings)
Structure, about 110 to 140 words:
1. Self-intro first: year, degree, university, plus in the same sentence what draws the user to this kind of work, specific to the field or firm.
2. Two or three short stories from facts_bank.md, each showing one of the user's traits without naming it. One number per story.
3. Close on one concrete task from the JD and the availability sentence.

Example shape (fill only with the user's own facts):
"I'm a second-year [degree] student at [university], and what draws me to [field] is [specific reason]. [Story 1 with one number.] [Story 2, told as a small moment.] I'd love to bring that to [one task from the JD], and I'm available [availability]."

## Cover letters
* Open with why this firm's kind of problem appeals, built around one real thing about the firm or team.
* One story told as a moment that maps to a JD task; say why it maps.
* What the user hopes to learn or contribute there; availability; polite close.
* Under about 300 words, 3 or 4 short paragraphs. Vary openings across letters.

## Emails
* "Dear Mr/Ms [Surname]," then "I hope this email finds you well."
* Softeners: "I would be grateful for the chance to…", "I would be very happy to…".
* Before the thanks, ask for the opportunity: "I would sincerely appreciate the opportunity to discuss how I could contribute to your team, and I would be happy to provide any further information you may need."
* Close: "Please find my CV attached for your reference. Thank you very much for your time and consideration, and I look forward to hearing from you."

## Avoid
"I am writing to", "I am particularly drawn to", "passionate", "synergy", "leverage", stacked figures, tool lists inside a story, generic praise such as "your esteemed firm".
